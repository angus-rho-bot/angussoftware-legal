AI Disclosure

Angus Software — Portland, Oregon. Last updated: October 3, 2026.

This page describes how artificial intelligence tools are used in the software we build and publish, and states our position on authorship of creative work.

The short version

  • AI tools assist in writing code. We use Z.ai GLM models, driven by Letta agents, to draft, refactor, and review code across our projects.
  • All copy and copyrightable material is authored by humans. Marketing copy, documentation prose, artwork, and any other creative or copyrightable expression is written and selected by people. AI may assist with mechanics (formatting, proofreading), but the words, images, and decisions are ours.
  • Code is FOSS where it's published at all. Our public repositories carry standard open-source licenses (MIT or Apache-2.0). Anything we distribute is distributable.

Authorship & copyright policy

We follow the emerging consensus reflected in the U.S. Copyright Office's guidance on AI-assisted works: material produced with AI assistance under human direction, selection, and arrangement is human-authored and copyrightable; purely machine-generated content is not treated as authored material by us, and we do not claim copyright over it.

In practice: every piece of prose, artwork, music, or other copyrightable expression associated with our projects is sourced from humans. Where AI tools contributed to functional code, that code is released under standard open-source licenses so anyone can use, inspect, and redistribute it.

Client and third-party sites we host (such as Fountain Park Apts.) follow the same principle: code is AI-assisted and MIT-licensed; all band content, artwork, and copy belong to the artist.

Why disclose?

Because it's true, and because pretending AI isn't in the toolchain would be worse. We think the honest position is: state what the tools did, keep authorship of creative work with humans, and make the functional output as open as its license allows.
